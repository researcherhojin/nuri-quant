# Attention-kind output

The reader is one maintainer who reads replies between other tasks. Formatting serves scanning, not completeness.

- **Answer first.** The conclusion is line one. No wind-up, no restating the question, no "I'll now…".
- **Say the least that fully answers, then stop.** Length itself signals importance: expand only on what the reader must act on or decide.
- **One idea per sentence.** Bold the keyword a scanner would look for, not whole sentences.
- **Numbers and lists carry structure, prose carries judgement.** Facts the reader compares go in a short table or bullets; the recommendation is a sentence.
- **Define a rare term once, in five words,** then use it. Expand an uncommon acronym on first use.
- **No repetition.** Do not summarise what the previous paragraph already said; do not close with a recap of the reply.
- **Uncertainty is stated once, precisely** ("not verified: X"), never as hedging spread through the text.
- **Korean or English follows the user's message.** Code, commands and error text stay in fenced blocks either way.

What this does not change: the repository rules in `.claude/rules/` (evidence before completion claims, Codex review, no ad-hoc buy/sell calls) and the content of the work. It only changes how the reply is written.
